# Recipe Secrets Website Requirements

Create a website for Recipe Secrets Restaurant.
It should be a simple website with a maximum of 5 page - Home, About, Contact, Reservation, Specials.

It will be hosted on a free hosting site, so use Next.js or plan vanilla HTML files only.

## Project: Recipe Secrets Restaurant Website

Act as a senior frontend developer and UI/UX designer. Build a complete, modern, visually stunning website for Recipe Secrets Restaurant. The website should look professionally designed, feel welcoming and premium, and encourage visitors to explore the restaurant and make reservations.

Visual appeal is the highest priority. Do not create a generic template or a plain, text-heavy website. Build a beautiful, image-rich restaurant experience with polished layouts and excellent attention to detail.

1. Technology and Hosting
   Use Next.js with static export or vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Choose the simplest appropriate option.
   The website must be deployable on free hosting platforms such as GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages, or Netlify.
   Avoid paid APIs, paid hosting, unnecessary dependencies, and backend requirements.
   Keep the website fast, lightweight, maintainable, and easy to deploy.
   If using Next.js, ensure all pages work with static export.
2. Pages and Navigation

Create exactly five main pages:

Home (/)
About (/about)
Contact (/contact)
Reservation (/reservation)
Specials (/specials)

Use a consistent navigation bar, responsive mobile menu, active-page indicators, and a polished footer across all pages. Do not create additional main pages.

3. Design and Visual Requirements

Create a premium restaurant aesthetic inspired by high-end dining websites.

Use a sophisticated color palette featuring warm cream, off-white, charcoal, deep olive green, and subtle gold accents.
Use beautiful typography, generous whitespace, strong visual hierarchy, elegant spacing, and refined details.
Make the website visually engaging with large imagery, attractive layouts, tasteful animations, smooth transitions, and subtle hover effects.
Ensure every page looks professionally designed and maintains consistent branding.
Make the website fully responsive across mobile, tablet, and desktop.
Avoid clutter, excessive animations, generic templates, and repetitive layouts.
Images Are Essential

Use high-quality, appetizing food photography throughout the website. Do not leave blank image areas or rely on text alone.

Home: A stunning hero image, featured dishes, restaurant atmosphere, and food highlights.
About: Restaurant ambience, cooking, ingredients, and dining photography.
Specials: Individual, high-quality images for featured dishes and promotions.
Contact and Reservation: Appropriate supporting photography that complements the content without distracting from forms.

Source suitable, publicly accessible images from reliable sources such as Unsplash or Pexels. Use different relevant images for different sections, with professional cropping, consistent aspect ratios, responsive sizing, meaningful alt text, and fallbacks. Verify that image URLs work. Respect image licensing requirements and optimize images for fast loading.

The finished website should feel like a real, established restaurant brand that makes visitors excited to dine there.

4. Page Content and Features
   Home
   Attractive full-width hero section with food photography, compelling headline, short introduction, and prominent Make a Reservation and Explore Specials buttons.
   Restaurant introduction and unique selling points.
   Featured dishes with images, descriptions, and prices where available.
   Specials preview, location and opening-hours preview, and footer.
   About
   Restaurant story, philosophy, ingredients, food preparation, hospitality, and dining experience.
   Beautiful photography and an engaging editorial-style layout.
   Use editable placeholders for unknown business details. Do not invent historical claims, awards, or credentials.
   Contact
   Address, phone, email, opening hours, and social links when supplied.
   Contact form with name, email, subject, message, validation, and feedback.
   Map or directions section if the actual address is provided.
   Clickable email and telephone links.
   Reservation
   Form fields for name, email, phone, date, preferred time, number of guests, and optional special requests.
   Validate required fields, input formats, and reservation dates. Prevent selecting past dates.
   Provide clear submission feedback and a polished, mobile-friendly form.
   Specials
   Attractive image-based cards for featured dishes, seasonal offerings, and promotions.
   Each card should support an image, name, description, price, category, and availability period where applicable.
   Store specials in an easily editable data structure.
   Include a clear reservation call to action.

Do not invent real restaurant details, menu prices, promotions, or contact information. Use clearly labeled sample content where necessary.

5. Functionality
   Ensure all navigation links, buttons, and calls to action work.
   Implement responsive navigation and accessible interactive components.
   Add client-side form validation and clear success or error messages.
   Since static hosting cannot independently process submissions or verify table availability, provide a practical integration point or email-based fallback for contact and reservation forms.
   Do not falsely claim a reservation is confirmed or a message has been sent when it has not.
   Keep form integrations modular so a real booking provider or form service can be connected later.
6. Quality, Accessibility, and SEO
   Use reusable components and clean, organized code.
   Use semantic HTML, proper form labels, keyboard accessibility, visible focus states, sufficient contrast, and reduced-motion support.
   Add page-specific titles, meta descriptions, favicon, meaningful image alt text, and Open Graph metadata where practical.
   Optimize images, minimize unnecessary JavaScript, and avoid horizontal overflow.
   Ensure there are no broken links, missing images, console errors, or non-functional buttons.
7. Implementation and Delivery
   Inspect the existing project before making changes.
   Choose the appropriate technology and establish the visual design system.
   Build and style all five pages completely.
   Source and integrate suitable images throughout the website.
   Implement navigation, forms, validation, and static-hosting-compatible fallbacks.
   Test the website on mobile, tablet, and desktop.
   Run available build and lint commands, fix errors, and verify the production build.
   Review the actual rendered pages and improve anything that looks unfinished or visually weak.
   Provide a README with installation, development, build, deployment, and content-update instructions.
   Final Requirements

The deliverable must be a complete, polished, visually impressive, image-rich, functional five-page restaurant website, not just a mockup, wireframe, or implementation plan.

Prioritize beautiful food imagery, professional UI/UX, responsive layouts, consistent branding, fast performance, and ease of deployment on free hosting.

If details are missing, make sensible design decisions and use clearly labeled placeholders. Do not stop to ask unnecessary questions. Start by inspecting the repository, then implement the entire website and verify that it builds successfully.
